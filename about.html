about

Computer engineer by training, AI engineer by trade.

Where the machine learning work meets the systems work.

Background

I'm a Computer Engineering graduate from the University of Jordan (GPA 3.77, Excellent), working as an AI Research & Integration Engineer at Penguin Location Services in Amman. My route into AI came through machine learning on real sensor data — training and comparing models that estimate where something physically is from Bluetooth signal readings, where the input is noisy and nothing arrives clean.

That start shaped how I work. I care about what a model does when the input is messy, what it does when it isn't sure, and whether the whole thing still runs next month — not just whether it scored well in a notebook.

What I do today

My work sits across three areas that feed each other. Machine learning: classification and regression on real data, ensembles, and neural networks in TensorFlow and Keras, including the question of how a model should behave when the input doesn't belong to any of its classes. Computer vision: object detection and image classification, from collecting the dataset through to a deployed app. And large language models: RAG, embeddings, vector databases, tool-calling agents, and local models through Ollama.

Around all of it sits automation — the part that takes a model or an agent and connects it to the databases, APIs and messaging platforms where it becomes useful to somebody. I taught myself that side independently, and it's now where a lot of my day goes.

Engineering background

My degree covered the hardware side as well — processor design in Verilog, embedded C and assembly on microcontrollers. I don't build in that area day to day, but it's where I learned to think about systems at a low level, and it's part of why deployment and reliability come naturally rather than as an afterthought. A few of those projects are on the Projects page.
